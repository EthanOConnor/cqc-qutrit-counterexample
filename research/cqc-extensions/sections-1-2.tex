\section{Summary of the main conclusions}

The qutrit construction is not isolated. A rank-two family exists in every equal local dimension $d\ge2$ for the computational and Fourier mutually unbiased bases (MUBs), with exact CQC gap
\begin{equation}
\Delta_d(\eps)=\hbin(\eps)-2\hbin(\eps/d).
\end{equation}
At $\eps=1/10$, this family violates CQC for every $d\ge3$, and the violation increases with $d$. The same family has an exact no-go at $d=2$.

No unrestricted two-qubit counterexample has been found in the searches reported here. The original large random search, the known proved cases, and supplementary exploratory searches all point toward the possibility that the qubit case survives as a separate theorem, but no general proof is supplied here \cite{Schneeloch2014}.

Even after CQC fails, several universal replacement bounds remain. For two local MUB pairs,
\begin{equation}
I_Z+I_X\le \min\{\log_2 d-H(A\mid B),\ \log_2d-H(B\mid A)\}.
\end{equation}
For $m$ pairwise MUB measurement pairs,
\begin{equation}
\sum_{i=1}^{m} I(M_i^A:M_i^B)
\le \frac{m}{2}\min\{\log_2 d-H(A\mid B),\ \log_2d-H(B\mid A)\}.
\end{equation}
These bounds also yield useful entropy- and purity-dependent corollaries.

\section{An all-dimension rank-two family}

\subsection{Logical qubit inside $\C^d$}

For $d\ge2$, define
\begin{equation}
\ket{0_L}=\ket{0},
\qquad
\ket{1_L}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d-1}}\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\ket{j},
\end{equation}
and set
\begin{equation}
 c_d=\frac{1}{\sqrt d},
 \qquad
 s_d=\sqrt{\frac{d-1}{d}}.
\end{equation}
Define two orthogonal, maximally entangled states on this logical two-dimensional support:
\begin{align}
\ket{\phi_d}
 &=\frac{\ket{0_L0_L}+\ket{1_L1_L}}{\sqrt2},\\
\ket{\chi_d}
 &=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\left[
 s_d\bigl(\ket{0_L0_L}-\ket{1_L1_L}\bigr)
 -c_d\bigl(\ket{0_L1_L}+\ket{1_L0_L}\bigr)
 \right].
\end{align}
Mix them as
\begin{equation}
\rho_{d,\eps}=(1-\eps)\proj{\phi_d}+\eps\proj{\chi_d},
\qquad 0\le\eps\le1.
\end{equation}
For $d=3$, this is the qutrit counterexample already established.

Both components have maximally mixed logical marginals, so
\begin{equation}
\rho_A=\rho_B=\frac12\Pi_L,
\end{equation}
where $\Pi_L$ projects onto $\operatorname{span}\{\ket{0_L},\ket{1_L}\}$. The nonzero eigenvalues of $\rho_{d,\eps}$ are $1-\eps$ and $\eps$, hence
\begin{equation}
I(A:B)=2-\hbin(\eps).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Computational-basis mutual information}

After coarse-graining physical outcomes $1,\ldots,d-1$ into one logical outcome, the computational measurement has binary joint distribution
\begin{equation}
P_{\rm logical}=
\begin{pmatrix}
\frac12-\frac{\eps}{2d} & \frac{\eps}{2d}\\[2mm]
\frac{\eps}{2d} & \frac12-\frac{\eps}{2d}
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
To see explicitly why the full $d$-outcome measurement has the same mutual information, write
\begin{equation}
 a=\frac12-\frac{\eps}{2d},
 \qquad
 b=\frac{\eps}{2d}.
\end{equation}
Its fine-grained probabilities are
\begin{align}
P_{00}&=a,\\
P_{0k}=P_{k0}&=\frac{b}{d-1}, && k>0,\\
P_{jk}&=\frac{a}{(d-1)^2}, && j,k>0.
\end{align}
Thus, conditioned on each coarse logical outcome, the physical labels are generated by independent local uniform refinements. Such refinements preserve mutual information. Therefore
\begin{equation}
I_Z=1-\hbin(\eps/d).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Fourier-basis mutual information}

Let
\begin{equation}
\ket{x_k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt d}\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\omega^{jk}\ket{j},
\qquad \omega=e^{2\pi i/d},
\end{equation}
be the Fourier basis. Its projectors compressed to the logical support are
\begin{align}
\Pi_L\proj{x_0}\Pi_L&=\proj{u},\\
\Pi_L\proj{x_k}\Pi_L&=\frac{1}{d-1}\proj{v}, && k>0,
\end{align}
where
\begin{equation}
\ket{u}=c_d\ket{0_L}+s_d\ket{1_L},
\qquad
\ket{v}=s_d\ket{0_L}-c_d\ket{1_L}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, $\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\omega^{jk}=d-1$ for $k=0$ and $-1$ for $k>0$. Hence the Fourier measurement is the logical $\{\ket{u},\ket{v}\}$ measurement, with the $v$ outcome split uniformly among $d-1$ physical labels.

Let
\begin{equation}
O_d=\begin{pmatrix}c_d&s_d\\s_d&-c_d\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
A direct calculation gives
\begin{equation}
(O_d\otimes O_d)\ket{\phi_d}=\ket{\phi_d},
\qquad
(O_d\otimes O_d)\ket{\chi_d}=-\ket{\chi_d}.
\end{equation}
The density operator is invariant under the logical basis change, so the logical joint table is the same as in the computational basis. The fine physical labels are again independent uniform refinements, and therefore
\begin{equation}
I_X=I_Z=1-\hbin(\eps/d).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Exact CQC gap and dimensional threshold}

Combining the preceding formulas gives
\begin{equation}
\Delta_d(\eps):=I_Z+I_X-I(A:B)=\hbin(\eps)-2\hbin(\eps/d).
\end{equation}
At $\eps=1/10$:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}rc@{}}
\toprule
$d$ & $\Delta_d(1/10)$ (bits)\\
\midrule
2 & $-0.103798320642631$\\
3 & $+0.047310992952217$\\
4 & $+0.131673730595941$\\
5 & $+0.186114508505640$\\
6 & $+0.224412399450532$\\
10 & $+0.307409321797459$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
For $d=3$ this is the qutrit gap. Since $\hbin(1/(10d))$ decreases with $d\ge3$, the violation grows for higher dimensions.

An exact integer sign certificate is
\begin{equation}
10d\,\Delta_d(1/10)
=
\log_2\!\left[
\frac{(10d-1)^{20d-2}}{10^{10d}d^{20d}3^{18d}}
\right].
\end{equation}
Thus
\begin{equation}
\Delta_d(1/10)>0
\iff
(10d-1)^{20d-2}>10^{10d}d^{20d}3^{18d}.
\end{equation}
At $d=3$ this reduces to $29^{58}>3^{114}10^{30}$.

Because the gap is strictly positive, sufficiently small white-noise perturbations
\begin{equation}
(1-\eta)\rho_{d,1/10}+\eta\frac{I_{d^2}}{d^2}
\end{equation}
are full-rank counterexamples for every $d\ge3$ by continuity.

\subsection{Dependence on the chosen MUB pair}

The computational/Fourier derivation extends to any prescribed pair of rank-one MUBs. The transition matrix between two MUBs is a complex Hadamard matrix. Rephase rows and columns so its first row and first column are all $1/\sqrt d$. Orthogonality then implies that, in every nonfirst column, the sum of the remaining entries is $-1/\sqrt d$.

Choose $\ket{0_L}$ as the first vector of the first basis and $\ket{1_L}$ as the normalized uniform sum of the remaining vectors. The second basis then has exactly the same compressed projectors as above:
\begin{equation}
\proj{u},\qquad \frac{1}{d-1}\proj{v},\ldots,\frac{1}{d-1}\proj{v}.
\end{equation}
Applying this construction independently on the two local systems gives the same logical state and joint distributions. This appears to establish an adapted counterexample for every $d\ge3$ and every prescribed local MUB pair. Because this is a new extension beyond the original qutrit certificate, it should still be checked independently before being quoted as a theorem.
